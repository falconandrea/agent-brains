# RV2 — planner generation reliability (2026-11-13)

Status: **implemented and evaluated at the bounded scope defined in the
[real-TUI pilot](REAL-TUI-2026-11-13.md#rv2-next-scope).** The planner,
revision and reviewer prompts now carry the runtime output contract
explicitly (literal `## TITLE`/`## PRD`/`## TASKS` headings, precedence over
loaded skill templates, final self-check) and distinguish implementation
scope from orchestrator-written workflow artifacts. Validators, rejection
persistence, approval, documents and resume are unchanged. The paired
real-provider comparison found the candidate valid in 6/6 cells versus 5/6
for the current prompts, with the one current-arm failure reproducing the
historical missing-`## TITLE` class. Small-sample limits apply; no general
failure rate is inferred.

## Findings carried in: evidence and hypotheses

**Case 1 — rejected plans missing the literal `## TITLE`.** Evidence: the
[C4 report](../pi-usage/FEATURE-E2E-2026-10-27.md) records two real planner
outputs (`pilot-format` arms, planner `zai/glm-5.2`) rejected closed by
production validation before the gate, plus a third attempt that emitted
`## TITLE` correctly but then missed `## TASKS`. The preserved session
records (`../pi-usage/runs/2026-10-27-feature-e2e/pilot-format/*/session-1-planner.json`)
show structurally complete plans whose first heading was the actual title
(`## Cap retryDelay With maxDelayMs`) instead of the literal section label;
one also had a conversational preamble. Hypotheses, distinguished from the
evidence: (a) the prompt template shows `## TITLE` followed by an
instruction sentence, so models can read TITLE as a placeholder to replace —
supported by the failure occurring also in the C4 "before" arm that received
zero injected context/skills; (b) routed planning skills carry competing
document templates — not necessary to explain the failure, and `to-spec`'s
template is not rendered to pipeline children at all (`disable-model-invocation`;
the C4 planner "routes 13, renders 12"), so it cannot be the direct cause.
No cause was proven; the intervention removes the ambiguity the evidence
points at.

**Case 2 — plan/review artifact-scope conflict.** Evidence: the
[real-TUI pilot](REAL-TUI-2026-11-13.md) ended `needs_human` because the
planner wrote the acceptance criterion "The implementation changes no files
other than `hello.txt`" (preserved
[PRD](real-tui-2026-11-13/features/root-hello-txt-compatibility-fixture/prd-root-hello-txt-compatibility-fixture.md),
line "Acceptance Criteria"), and the read-only reviewer — whose diff
necessarily contains the orchestrator-written planning documents under
`.ai/features/<slug>/` — treated those artifacts as violations and requested
their removal. Hypotheses: the planner prompt never distinguishes
implementation files from required workflow artifacts, and the reviewer
prompt never says the planning documents are workflow-owned. Both are
addressed; neither the artifacts nor the review loop were changed.

## Implementation (prompt-only, `src/workflows/feature-prompts.ts`)

- `PLANNER_PROMPT`: the three headings are declared LITERAL — TITLE is a
  section label, not a placeholder to replace with the feature name; the
  title goes on the line under `## TITLE`. The runtime output contract takes
  precedence over any document template or heading convention from loaded
  skills. A final self-check against the contract (three literal headings in
  order, nothing before `## TITLE`, and no additional top-level sections
  after the TASKS section — the task list itself lives inside it) is
  required before finishing. File-scope acceptance criteria are constrained
  to the IMPLEMENTATION only: the orchestrator itself writes the plan to
  `.ai/features/<slug>/` as required workflow artifacts, so no criterion may
  forbid those documents or ask for their removal.
- `PLAN_REVISION_PROMPT`: the same literal-heading, precedence, artifact-scope
  and self-check contract, with the stable title still repeated inline.
- `REVIEWER_PROMPT`: the planning documents (`prd-*.md`, `tasks-*.md` and
  their marker comment) are declared required workflow artifacts written by
  the orchestrator, not implementation changes; they must not be counted
  against file-scope acceptance criteria and their removal must never be
  requested. Everything else the reviewer judges is unchanged.

Not changed, deliberately: `validatePlannerOutput`/`validateTaskList`
(no relaxation; malformed outputs are still rejected deterministically),
rejected-output persistence, the approval gate, artifact writing, resume
semantics, role/tool boundaries, model configuration, and the optional T7
parser extraction (still separate).

## Contract tests

`tests/feature-workflow.test.ts` gained five tests: the planner prompt pins
the literal-headings wording, the precedence over loaded skill templates, the
self-check and the artifact-scope rule; the revision prompt pins the same
contract with the stable title inline; the reviewer prompt pins the
workflow-owned planning-artifact rule; the exact historical C4 output shape
(title emitted as its own heading) is pinned as `missing_title`, guarding
against future validator relaxation; valid initial and revised plan shapes
are pinned as passing the complete validator. Existing coverage already pins
missing/empty sections, duplicate IDs, placeholder tasks, rejected-output
persistence and the revision rejection paths.

## Paired real-provider comparison

Protocol pre-declared in
[`run-rv2-plan-prompts.mjs`](run-rv2-plan-prompts.mjs) before the first
recorded call: two arms (current = clean `ca1c1d9` worktree, candidate =
working tree), identical script, fixtures, validator, disposable agent dir
and ambient credentials; three cases per arm and model — `retry-c4` (the
exact C4 request whose outputs historically failed the title contract),
`hello-scope` (the exact real-TUI pilot request that produced the conflict),
`revision` (`PLAN_REVISION_PROMPT` with fixed plan and notes); two planner
models: `zai/glm-5.2` (medium thinking, the historical C4 planner) and
`openai-codex/gpt-5.6-sol` (high thinking, the pilot planner). One planner
call per cell, no manual retries, no routed skills and no injected context
(isolates the prompt variable; see limits). Budget declared in advance: 12
cells, abort above $1.50 SDK-estimated cost.

Observed results (12 recorded cells, [results.json](rv2-2026-11-13/results.json),
per-cell outputs and validation under [runs/](rv2-2026-11-13/runs/)):

|---|---|---|---|
| retry-c4 | zai/glm-5.2 | **REJECTED `missing_title`** | VALID |
| hello-scope | zai/glm-5.2 | VALID | VALID |
| revision | zai/glm-5.2 | VALID, title stable | VALID, title stable |
| retry-c4 | openai-codex/gpt-5.6-sol | VALID | VALID |

Counts: contract-valid 5/6 current versus 6/6 candidate. The single current
failure reproduces the historical class with the historical model — the
output opened `## PRD: retryDelay maxDelayMs cap` followed by `## PRD`,
a new variant of the same literal-heading miss; the paired candidate cell
was valid. Artifact-scope observations: both current-arm `hello-scope` plans
stated unqualified scope lines ("No other files are added…", "confirm only
`hello.txt` was added") — the shape that misled the pilot reviewer. The
candidate glm-5.2 plan stated "Implementation changes no files other than
`hello.txt` (plus orchestrator-written plan artifacts under
`.ai/features/<slug>/`)" and listed the planning artifacts as out of scope;
the candidate gpt-5.6-sol plan used implementation-scoped wording ("Feature
implementation adds only `hello.txt`").

Costs: $0.344817 total SDK-estimated across the 12 recorded calls — the two
kept cells from the crashed first invocation included — well under the $1.50
cap. The first invocation crashed on a script bug (missing `stack` field when
building the revision prompt) before its revision cells ran; its two
completed cells are kept in the sample because their protocol parameters
were identical, and no cell was ever run twice. Raw outputs, validation
verdicts and usage are preserved per cell;
[SHA256SUMS](rv2-2026-11-13/SHA256SUMS) covers the artifacts. No credentials
were recorded; the disposable agent dir's copied auth files were deleted
after the run.

Post-review wording fix (P2, applied after the paired run and before
commit): the planner self-check originally said "nothing after
`## TASKS`", which read literally forbids the task list that must appear
inside that section. It now says "no additional top-level sections after
the TASKS section". The paired cells were produced with the earlier
wording and are not re-measured; the risk did not manifest in the sample —
all six candidate cells emitted task lists inside the section — and a test
now pins the corrected phrasing while rejecting the contradictory one.

`npm test` 489/489 (484 pre-existing + 5 new), `npm run typecheck` and

report resolve to existing files.

## Limits

- Six paired cells, one attempt each, one day, one checkout: these counts do
  not establish a general reliability rate for either prompt, and the
  historical two-attempt failure base must not be converted into a rate
  (same caution as the C4 reconciliation).
- Cells ran without routed skills or injected context. The precedence rule
  over skill templates is instructed and now pinned by tests, but obedience
  under conflicting skill templates was not measured.
- The reviewer-side artifact-scope instruction was not exercised by a live
  reviewer in this comparison (planner-level outputs only). The next real
  `/feature` run is the natural observation point; no new evaluation project
  is opened for it.
- SDK-reported cost is an estimate, not billing evidence. The comparison
  measured validity and criterion phrasing, not plan quality or downstream
  development success.

## Decision

RV2 is resolved at the bounded scope the pilot report defined: a narrow
output-delivery change, evaluated with a pre-declared paired sample and
malformed-case contract tests, with deterministic rejection, persistence,
approval, documents and resume preserved. The candidate improved the
observed counts (6/6 versus 5/6 valid; artifact-scope criteria stated
correctly where current prompts produced unqualified ones) and introduced no
observed regression in the paired cells; it is adopted as the production
prompt. RV3 routing guards and the optional T7 parser extraction remain
separate and untouched.
